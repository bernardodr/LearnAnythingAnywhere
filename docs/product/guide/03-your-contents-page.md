# 3. Your book's page

[← Starting a textbook](02-starting-a-textbook.md) · [Guide home](../README.md) · Next: [Reading a lesson →](04-reading-a-lesson.md)

Every textbook has its own page, set out like the front of a printed book: the cover, the title, a contents list of your lessons, and useful pages at the back. Open it by tapping the book in your library. The arrow at the top left takes you back to your library.

## At the top: the cover, the title and your goal

At the top is the book's cover, just as it looks in your library, with its subject drawing. Underneath:

- **The title.**
- **Your goal in one line:** the headline of your [mission](02-starting-a-textbook.md#4-your-mission-card).
- **A line of facts:** which volume this is in your library, how many lessons it has so far, and how many of them you've read (for example *Volume III · 2 lessons · 1 read*).

## Contents: your lessons

Under **Inside this book**, your lessons are listed like the contents of a book: numbered *01*, *02*, *03* and so on, with a fine line between each. On the right:

- **A tick:** you've finished that lesson.
- **Up next**, in green: the lesson to read now. Its number is green too.

Tap any lesson to open it.

**The button at the bottom of the screen** is the main thing to do next:

- **Start reading** opens your first lesson.
- **Continue reading** opens the lesson you're up to.
- **Write lesson *n*** appears once you've read every lesson so far, and writes the next one (see below).

## How new lessons arrive

LAA writes your textbook one lesson at a time, and each lesson is written *after* you've finished the one before. That way, each lesson can build on how you're getting on, rather than following a plan fixed on day one.

The last line of the contents shows what's next, in grey:

- **Your first lesson:** right after you confirm your mission, lesson 1 is written for you automatically.
- **Being written now:** a lesson is being written. You'll see what's happening underneath (*Choosing what to teach next*, *Writing lesson 2: …*, *Checking the quizzes and sources*). It usually takes about a minute. You can leave and come back; it carries on without you.
- **Written after lesson *n*:** finish lesson *n* first (see [Finishing a lesson](04-reading-a-lesson.md#finishing-a-lesson)). Then **Write lesson *n*+1** appears at the bottom of the screen; tap it to get your next lesson.

Every lesson is checked before you see it: for example, that its quiz answers aren't given away by how they're worded. If a lesson can't be written properly, you'll see a message saying nothing was saved; just tap **Write lesson** again.

## Why you're learning this

Below the contents, set in the margin with a green line beside it, is the *why* of your mission: the real-world reason you started this book, in your own words. It's there as a reminder whenever you come back.

## At the back of the book

Like a good textbook, yours has useful pages at the back. They fill up as you learn.

They're listed like the contents: **A**, **B**, **C** and **D**, each with how many things it holds on the right (like a page number), or *None yet*. Tap one to open it, and tap again to fold it away.

### A. Reference sheets

Each lesson comes with a **cheat sheet**: the essentials of that lesson on one short page, for quick reminders later. They're all collected here, each with the lesson it came from. Tap one to read it.

### B. Glossary

The words that matter in your subject, with short definitions. A word is added only once you've used it correctly yourself, in your answer to a practice question, so the glossary is a record of what *you* know, not a dictionary to study.

### C. What you've learned

Notes on what you've shown you can do. A note is added when you get a practice question fully right (see [Practice and your tutor](05-practice-and-your-tutor.md)). Reading through a lesson isn't enough on its own; it takes showing you can use the idea. Future lessons are planned from these notes, so they build on what you've really mastered.

### D. Sources

Books, articles, videos and websites you trust for this subject.

**LAA never makes up sources.** It won't invent a book, a website or a study. So this list contains only the sources you add yourself, and lessons will only refer to sources on it.

**To add a source:**

1. Tap **D Sources** to open it, then tap **Add a source**.
2. Fill in its **Title** (for example *Book: The Self-Coached Climber*), its **Web address** (starting with `https://`) and, if you like, **What it's good for** (for example *Use for: footwork drills*).
3. Tap **Add source**.

If your [reader settings](07-reader-settings.md#your-settings) turn links **Off**, your sources are listed without their web addresses, and **Add a source** isn't available.

From your next lesson on, LAA can point you to your sources: a small reference like *[1]* after a paragraph that draws on one, and a **Primary source** box suggesting the best one to read or watch.
