# 2. Starting a textbook

[← Getting started](01-getting-started.md) · [Guide home](../README.md) · Next: [Your contents page →](03-your-contents-page.md)

Every textbook starts the same way: you name a topic, LAA checks it, and then a short interview turns your reason for learning it into a **mission**. Your lessons are written around that mission.

## 1. Choose a topic

Tap **+** on your shelf (or one of the **Start with** suggestions). On **What would you like to learn?**, type your topic in the **Topic** box (a skill, a subject or a hobby) and tap **Begin the preface**.

Keep it simple: *bouldering*, *watercolour painting*, *Python*, *how bread rises*. You'll get to say exactly what you want out of it in a moment.

## 2. The topic check

Before anything is written, LAA checks that the topic is something it can write a good, safe textbook about. One of three things happens:

- **It goes ahead.** Almost every topic does. LAA may tidy the title (*bouldering* becomes *Bouldering*).
- **It's approached in a different way.** For some topics, LAA suggests a version that keeps what's worth learning. You'll see a short note explaining the approach at the top of the next screen, and your textbook takes that title.
- **It's declined.** For topics LAA won't write about, you'll see a kind message instead, often with an idea for something related you could learn. Nothing is created, so just try another topic.

The check also follows your [reader settings](07-reader-settings.md). If a topic touches on something your settings leave out (gambling, say, with child-safe mode on), the message tells you so and suggests other things to learn.

If you see *"We couldn't check this topic just now, so nothing was created"*, the check couldn't run. Try again in a moment. LAA never skips the check.

## 3. The preface: why do you want to learn this?

Your textbook opens with its **Preface**: a short conversation with your tutor about *why* you want to learn the topic.

**Why it matters:** "get better at baking" can't tell anyone what to teach first; "bake a sourdough loaf good enough to take to a dinner party next month" can. A clear goal makes every lesson useful.

**How it works:**

- The tutor asks its first question as soon as the preface opens. Each reply appears in full once it's been written and checked against your [reader settings](07-reader-settings.md); until then you'll see *Your tutor is writing…*
- Type in **Your answer** and tap **Send**. You'll see *Question 1 of up to 5* beneath the box.
- Expect two to five questions. If an answer is vague, the tutor will gently ask you to be more concrete, for example what success would look like, or by when.

**Tips for good answers:**

- Say what you'd love to be able to *do*, not just "understand" ("climb a V3 with my friends", "order dinner in Spanish").
- Mention a deadline if you have one, and how much time you can give it.
- Say what you *don't* want to cover, if anything ("no outdoor climbing for now").

You can leave and come back. Your answers are saved, and the preface picks up where you stopped.

## 4. Your mission card

When the tutor has enough, it thanks you and shows **Your mission**:

- **The headline:** your goal in one line. It's what appears under the cover on your shelf.
- **Why:** the real-world reason you're learning this.
- **Success looks like:** the things you'll be able to do.
- **Limits:** your time, budget or other constraints.
- **Not now:** related things you've chosen to leave out for now.

**Check it carefully.** Every lesson will be planned from it.

- To change something, tap **Edit**, change any field, then tap **Done** (or **Cancel** to keep the original). In the list fields, put one item per line.
- When you're happy, tap **Write my textbook**. Your mission is saved and your first lesson is written straight away.

Once you've tapped **Write my textbook**, the mission can't be changed in this version.
