# 1. Getting started

[← Guide home](../README.md) · Next: [Starting a textbook →](02-starting-a-textbook.md)

## Creating your account

When you open LAA you'll see the title page: *"A textbook for whatever you're curious about."* The first time, make an account. **Accounts are for adults.** A child uses a parent's account, with reader settings the parent chooses for them and locks with a PIN (see [Letting a child use your account](07-reader-settings.md#letting-a-child-use-your-account)).

1. Tap **New here? Create an account**.
2. Type **your name** (optional) and your **email** address.
3. Choose a **password** (see [Choosing a password](#choosing-a-password)).
4. Tap **Create account**. You land on your library, already signed in.
5. **Confirm your email.** LAA sends you an email with a link: open it. Until you do, you can look around, but starting a textbook (or anything else that uses AI) waits. If the email doesn't come, check your spam folder, or tap **Send the link again** in your library. A link works for 24 hours.

Confirming your email shows the account really is yours, and it's how you can get back in if you forget your password.


## Signing in

1. Type your **email** and **password**.
2. Tap **Sign in**.

If either is wrong, you'll see *"Email or password is incorrect."* LAA doesn't say which, so nobody can use the sign-in page to find out who has an account.

**Too many tries:** after 5 wrong passwords for one email, signing in to it pauses for 15 minutes, even with the right password. Lots of wrong tries from one network (a home or a school, say) pause signing in from there too. This stops anyone from guessing their way in. Wait, then try again.

## Choosing a password

- Use **at least 15 characters**. The form counts them as you type.
- **Three or four random words** work well and are easy to remember, like a short, odd phrase. Spaces and any characters are fine; you don't need symbols or numbers.
- LAA turns down passwords that many people use, your own email address, and one character repeated, and says why.
- Tap **Show** next to a password to see what you've typed. Password managers work too.

## Your password

To change your password, open your settings (the settings icon at the top right of your library) and go to **Your password**. Type your **Current password** and a **New password** (and the **Parent PIN**, if you've set one), then tap **Change password**. You stay signed in where you made the change; anywhere else you were signed in asks you to sign in again. So if you think someone else knows your password, changing it also signs them out.

**If your account has no password yet** (it was made before passwords arrived), **Your password** asks you to set one instead.

## Forgotten your password?

1. On the title page, tap **Forgotten your password?**
2. Type your email and tap **Send reset link**. LAA says it has sent a link if that email has an account. It says the same either way, so nobody can use it to find out who has an account.
3. Open the link in the email (it works once, for 30 minutes) and choose a **New password**.
4. Tap **Save new password**. You're signed in, and signed out everywhere else.

If several reset emails are asked for in a short time, LAA waits a while before sending more.

## Your library

After you sign in, you land on **Your library**: your textbooks, laid out like a small, carefully kept bookcase.

- **Each textbook is a book.** Its cover is cloth in forest green, ochre, dusty blue or terracotta, with the title in large type and one small drawing of its subject: stars for the night sky, a leaf for plants, a mountain for climbing. LAA chooses the drawing when you finish the book's preface, from what the book is about.
- **A book with a red ribbon** sticking out of the top, and *Preface in progress* underneath, is one whose opening interview you haven't finished yet.
- **Tap a book to open it.** An unfinished one takes you back to its interview; a finished one opens [its page](03-your-contents-page.md): the cover, your lessons and your mission.
- **Create a textbook**, at the bottom of the screen, starts a new one. See [Starting a textbook](02-starting-a-textbook.md).
- **The settings icon** (two small sliders, at the top right) opens your settings: how your textbooks are written, your parent PIN, your password, and **Sign out**. See [Reader settings and the parent PIN](07-reader-settings.md).

### Covers or spines

Above your books, choose how your library looks:

- **Covers** shows each book's front cover, two to a row on a phone.
- **Spines** stands your books side by side with their spines facing out, like a real bookcase, so you can see many more at once. Each spine shows the title and volume number, and books differ a little in thickness and height. A book whose preface you haven't finished has its red ribbon.

The one you're looking at is underlined. LAA remembers your choice on each device. Tap a spine to open its book, just like a cover.

### Arranging your library

Put your books in whatever order you like, by hand:

- **On a computer**, drag a book to where you want it.
- **On a phone or tablet**, press and hold a book until it lifts, then drag it.

As you move a book, the others slide quickly aside to make room, and the book leans a little as you carry it. Let go, and it drops straight into its place and stays there, with a small knock against the books beside it. Your order is kept for you, so it's the same on every device. New textbooks go at the end.

**Prefer not to drag?** Tap **Arrange** above your books. With covers, each book gets **‹** and **›** buttons underneath to move it one place earlier or later. With spines, tap a book to choose it (it rises a little), then use **‹ Earlier** and **Later ›**. Tap **Done** when you're finished. With a screen reader, each book also has *Move earlier* and *Move later* actions.

**Why it helps:** it's your library. Keep the book you're working on first, group books that belong together, or just enjoy putting things in order.

If you've asked your device for less motion, books still move, without the lean and the knock.

### When your library is empty

A new library shows a dotted outline where your first book will stand, some **Start with** suggestions (like *Bouldering* or *Sourdough bread*), and **Create a textbook** at the bottom.

- Tap a suggestion to start a textbook on that topic straight away.
- Or tap **Create a textbook** to type your own topic.

See [Starting a textbook](02-starting-a-textbook.md) for what happens next.

## Staying signed in

LAA remembers you on each device, so you don't need to sign in every time you open it. It keeps you signed in for about half a day, then asks again. Changing your password signs you out everywhere else straight away.

To sign out, open your settings (the settings icon at the top right of your library) and tap **Sign out** at the bottom.

## Phones, tablets and computers

LAA works the same way in a web browser and on a phone or tablet. Your textbooks, lessons and progress are kept for you, not on the device, so you can read a lesson on your laptop and carry on with it on your phone.
