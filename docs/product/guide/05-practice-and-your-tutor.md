# 5. Practice and your tutor

[← Reading a lesson](04-reading-a-lesson.md) · [Guide home](../README.md) · Next: [Good to know →](06-good-to-know.md)

Every lesson ends with a **practice question** you answer in your own words. A personal tutor reads your answer, tells you how close you are, and helps you get the rest of the way **without giving you the answer**.

> *Your tutor won't hand you the answer, but it will help you find it.*

**Why it works this way:** working something out for yourself, with a nudge when you need it, is how a skill really sticks. Being told the answer feels quicker, but it's quickly forgotten.

## Answering a practice question

1. Read the question in the **Practice** section of the lesson.
2. Write your answer in **Your answer**. A few sentences is usually right. Use your own words; there's no need to match the lesson's wording.
3. Tap **Send to your tutor**.

A few seconds later, the **Tutor's note** appears under your answer.

## Reading the tutor's note

**The label in the corner** tells you where you stand:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Met** | Your answer covers everything a full answer needs. Well done. |
| **Partly there** | You've got some of it, but something's still missing. |
| **Not yet** | Your answer hasn't reached the key ideas yet. Keep going. |

**The checklist** shows what your answer covers:

- **✓** next to each idea you've got, so you know what's already right.
- **○ One more point to find** (or *2 more points…*): how much is still missing. It doesn't say *what's* missing; that's for you to work out.

**The tutor's message**, in italics, says what you did well and then asks a question or gives a nudge towards what's missing.

## Improving your answer

- Tap **Revise answer** to jump back to your answer. Change it, then tap **Send your revised answer**. You can try as many times as you like.
- **Need a nudge?** After your first answer, a **Hint 1 of 3** button appears in the tutor's note. Hints come one at a time, from gentle to more direct, so you only take as much help as you need. They stay on the page, so you can reread them.
- Once you've used all three hints, try again with what they've told you, or ask your tutor in the chat below the lesson.

Sometimes, instead of a message, the note shows **Hint *n* of 3**. That means the tutor's own reply would have given too much away, so you get the next prepared hint instead.

## When you get it

When the label says **Met**, the tutor confirms what you did well and suggests how to use it next time.

LAA also makes a note of it. Shortly afterwards, under **What you've learned** at the back of the book, you'll find a short note on what you've shown you can do. Words you used correctly may be added to your **Glossary**. Your future lessons are planned from these notes. See [At the back of the book](03-your-contents-page.md#at-the-back-of-the-book).

Answering the same question correctly again won't add a second note.

## Chatting with your tutor

Every lesson has a **Stuck or curious?** box near the end, where you can ask your tutor anything about the lesson.

- Tap one of the example questions, or type your own in **Your question** and tap **Ask your tutor**.
- The reply appears word by word. Your conversation is kept with the lesson, so it's still there when you come back.

**What your tutor is good for:**

- Explaining something again, a different way, or with another example, ideally one from your own goal.
- Questions the lesson raised: *why does this work?*, *what if…?*
- Real-world questions it can give a view on, while suggesting you try it yourself or ask people who do it.

**What it won't do:** answer the practice question or the quizzes for you. Ask it to, and it will kindly give you a smaller step to take instead.
