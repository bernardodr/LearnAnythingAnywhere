# 4. Reading a lesson

[← Your book's page](03-your-contents-page.md) · [Guide home](../README.md) · Next: [Practice and your tutor →](05-practice-and-your-tutor.md)

Each lesson is one page you scroll through, and is meant to take just a few minutes. It teaches **one thing**, linked to your mission, which you can use straight away.

## The parts of a lesson

Lessons follow the same pattern, so you always know where you are.

**At the top**
- **The lesson number and title**, with a one-line summary underneath.
- **Your win today:** what you'll be able to do by the end. Below it, **For your mission** reminds you which goal this lesson serves.

**Warm-up** (from lesson 2 on)
- One or two quick questions about an earlier lesson. *Answer from memory.* Recalling something, rather than rereading it, is what makes it stick.

**The lesson itself**
- Short sections, each with a heading, explaining just what you need to know.
- **Key idea:** the one sentence to remember, marked with a line down the side.
- **Watch out for this habit:** a common mistake and how to avoid it. You may also see a **Note** with a useful extra.
- **Diagrams**, where a picture helps: see [Interactive diagrams](#interactive-diagrams) below.

**Check yourself**
- Quick questions to check you've understood. See [Quizzes](#quizzes) below.

**Steps to try**
- When the topic calls for it, a box of numbered steps (titled, for example, *Try it today*) for trying the skill for real, at the gym, in the kitchen or at the keyboard.

**Practice**
- A question you answer in your own words, checked by your tutor. See [Practice and your tutor](05-practice-and-your-tutor.md).

**Primary source**
- If you've [added sources](03-your-contents-page.md#d-sources), the best one to read or watch next. Otherwise you'll see a small *No sources yet* note.

**Stuck or curious?**
- Ask your tutor anything about the lesson. See [Chatting with your tutor](05-practice-and-your-tutor.md#chatting-with-your-tutor).

## Interactive diagrams

Some ideas are easier to *see*: how the parts of something fit together, the stages of a process, or what changes when one thing changes. When a lesson has one of those, it can include an **interactive diagram** (at most two per lesson). It's drawn for your lesson, in its own words, and you explore it right there on the page:

- **Tap a part** to read what it does.
- **Step through** a process with **Next** and **Back**.
- **Compare** two things side by side.
- **Move a slider** and watch what changes.

The line in italics under each diagram tells you what to try.

**Prefer reading?** Tap **Show as text** under any diagram to see the same explanation written out, and **Show the diagram** to go back. Screen readers can use the text version too. If a diagram can't be shown on your device, the lesson shows the text version by itself, with a note saying so.

**Safe by design:** a diagram can't go online, open links, or see anything else in LAA or on your device. Every word in it is checked against your reader settings before it reaches you, like the rest of the lesson. If a diagram doesn't pass, it's simply left out.

## Quizzes

Warm-up and *Check yourself* questions give instant feedback:

- Tap the answer you think is right. A green highlight marks the right answer, and your answer turns red if it wasn't.
- You'll see **Right.** or **Not quite.**, then a short explanation of *why*. Read it either way.
- **Your first choice counts.** After you've answered, the question locks. This keeps the quiz honest: it shows what you really remembered, which is the point of the exercise.

The answers are shuffled and written to be similar in length and wording, so you can't spot the right one by how it looks; you have to know it.

## Cheat sheet

Near the end of each lesson, tap **Cheat sheet: …** to open its one-page summary. It's also saved under *Reference sheets* at the back of the book, for later.

## Finishing a lesson

When you've worked through the lesson, tap **I've finished this lesson** at the bottom.

- This ticks the lesson off on your book's page.
- It unlocks your next lesson. If the next one is already written, you're taken straight to it. If not, you go back to your book's page, where you can tap **Write lesson *n*** at the bottom to get it.

Finish a lesson when you've really done it, ideally including the practice question, because your next lesson is planned from how you're getting on.

## Moving around

- **‹ Contents** at the top takes you back to your book's page.
- At the bottom, **‹ Lesson *n*** and **Lesson *n* ›** move to the previous or next lesson.
